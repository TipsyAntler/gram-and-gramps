1943–46World War II
U.S. Navy Armed Guard

Gramps serves aboard merchant ships.

Family records and his obituary identify Sam as a U.S. Navy Signalman 2nd Class in the Naval Armed Guard — Navy personnel assigned aboard merchant vessels. The recording carries him from San Francisco to the South Pacific and eventually British Columbia.

The first family file is explicitly titled “1943 Boot camp leave.jpg”; the second is the surviving “Missing Hat 1944.jpg” portrait.

1945Aug. 14 · likely
Vancouver

Japan surrenders. Gramps loses the hat.

Gramps remembers his ship reaching New Westminster as Japan surrendered, then heading into Vancouver for the celebration. Vancouver archival photographs strongly place those city crowds on August 14, 1945. The ship itself is still an open research question.

Archive file: “Missing Hat 1944.jpg.” The filename date and the likely V-J Day event date do not perfectly match, so both are preserved rather than forced together.

Hoboken Post Office

Sam becomes Postmaster.

After joining the Hoboken Post Office after the war and working his way through the ranks, Sam is appointed Hoboken Postmaster. The exact date comes from his obituary; independent USPS verification remains a future archive step.

2022October
The end of their shared lifetime

Sam and Marietta die five days apart.

Sam dies October 7 at 97. Marietta dies October 12 at 95. What survives is unusually rich: photographs, family records, the museum material, seventy-five years of shared history — and, most importantly for this project, their voices.
